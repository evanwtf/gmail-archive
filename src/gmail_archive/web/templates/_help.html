Searching

Type in the box at the top. Search covers the subject and body text of every message; use the operators below to search headers, dates and labels, which are not in the text index.

Who
from:amazon Sender contains this. Partial matches work — from:amazon finds order-update@amazon.com.
to:evan@example.com Any recipient contains this.
from:"john smith" Quote a value containing spaces.
When
before:2026-01-01 Sent before this date, exclusive.
after:2020-06-01 Sent on or after this date, inclusive.
on:2026-07-30 One calendar day, in UTC. The date box in the list header does the same thing with a calendar.
What
subject:invoice Subject line contains this.
label:"Bank Alerts" Carries this Gmail label, matched exactly. See all labels.
has:attachment Has at least one attachment.
is:unread is:starred Gmail's own state, as it was when the export was taken. Also is:read, is:important, is:sent, is:draft, is:spam, is:chat.
Text
"exact phrase" Words adjacent, in order.
invoice -receipt Has the first, not the second.
invoice or receipt Either one. Plain words are joined with "and".

Operators combine, and free text can sit alongside them: from:amazon has:attachment after:2025-01-01 refund

Worth knowing

  • Words are stemmed. Searching running also finds "run" and "runs". That is usually what you want, and occasionally surprising.
  • Dates are UTC. A message sent late in the evening local time can land on the next day here.
  • Results are newest first by default. Sort by relevance from the links above the results — relevance is slower, because it has to rank every match before it can pick the top ones.
  • Links are defanged. Every URL in an archived message is rewritten to hxxp:// so nothing can load or be clicked by accident. Nothing in this archive reaches the network.
  • Nothing here can change your mail. The archive is read-only: no sending, no deleting, no marking as read.

Getting around

  • The left rail is Gmail's own mailboxes and your top labels, with counts. All Mail is everything.
  • Unread, starred and important markers are Gmail's, as of the export.
  • Inside a message: View raw text shows the original RFC822 source, headers and all.
  • Statistics has archive-wide totals and the date range.
